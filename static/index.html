    Market cycle (Ray Dalio)

    Dalio's "economic machine": markets move through four regimes depending on whether growth and inflation are rising or falling, inside a short-term debt cycle (5–8 years) and a long-term debt cycle (50–75 years). The regime below is what markets are pricing now, read from 3- and 6-month momentum across stocks, bonds, gold and commodities (refreshed by Update finance data).     Position sizing

    Risk-first sizing

    Open-trade rules

    • Sell at the stop, no exceptions. The stop is never more than 8% below entry. Aim for an average loss nearer 5%.
    • Once the gain reaches twice the initial risk (2R), raise the stop to breakeven.
    • O'Neil: take profits at +20–25%. Exception: a stock that gains 20% within 3 weeks of its breakout is held for 8 weeks from the breakout.
    • Sell if the stock closes below the 50-day on heavy volume, or shows climax-top signs (largest gain of the run, exhaustion gap).

    Scan screened stocks

    Run the pattern finder across any list of stocks, such as the results of a Finviz screen, and rank the setups. Open a screen below (it opens in Finviz's tickers-only view), copy the tickers, paste them here and run the scan. Up to 100 tickers; it takes about a minute. Setups like a tight range sitting on flattening, converging 50/150/200-day averages after a long base ("MA squeeze coil") rank highest, followed by VCPs, flat bases, cups and flags that are ready or just breaking out.

    Pattern scan

    Patterns are found automatically in each stock's daily prices, using the rules from Identifying Chart Patterns (Fidelity / Kirkpatrick) plus O'Neil's cup with handle and Minervini's VCP. A pattern isn't complete until price breaks out of it, and a breakout counts as confirmed here only with a close at least 1% past the line on 140%+ of average volume. Treat this as a first pass and check it on your own chart.
